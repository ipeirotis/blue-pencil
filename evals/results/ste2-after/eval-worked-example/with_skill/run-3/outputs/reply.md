## 2. Revised text

```
The proliferation of online platforms has transformed how consumers make purchasing decisions. Several researchers have investigated the relationship between online reviews and sales (Chevalier and Mayzlin 2006; Forman et al. 2008; Dellarocas 2003). These studies found that reviews matter: a one-star increase raises revenue by 5-9%. However, to the best of our knowledge, whether reviewer identity affects this relationship remains underexplored.

To investigate this question, we use a dataset of 1.2 million reviews. Reviewer identity has a significant effect on this relationship. Platform managers should therefore consider highlighting reviewer credentials.
```

- **Opening sentence.** "It is important to note that the proliferation ... fundamentally transformed the way in which" became "The proliferation ... transformed how". This removes a banned throat-clearing opener and a wordy phrase, and the sentence now opens on its topic. "Fundamentally" is cut as an intensifier, since "transformed" already carries the claim.
- **Prior research.** "Furthermore, an investigation of the relationship ... was conducted by several researchers" became "Several researchers have investigated the relationship ...". This removes a banned connective, turns the nominalization into a verb, and puts the researchers in the subject slot. The citations are unchanged, in the same order, still at the end of the sentence.
- **The 5-9% finding.** "Moreover, these studies found that reviews matter, a one-star increase leads to a 5-9% increase in revenue" became "These studies found that reviews matter: a one-star increase raises revenue by 5-9%." This removes the banned connective and fixes the comma splice. The colon makes the number the payoff in stress position. The figures 5-9% and "one-star" are untouched, but the wording around them changed, so it is flagged below.
- **The gap claim.** "Crucially, however, the question of whether the identity of reviewers affects this relationship remains, to the best of our knowledge, somewhat underexplored in the existing literature" became "However, to the best of our knowledge, whether reviewer identity affects this relationship remains underexplored."
  - "Crucially" is banned and is removed.
  - The subject clause is reunited with its verb.
  - "However" stays because the contrast is real, which surfaces the turn.
  - "To the best of our knowledge" is kept as the claim's one hedge.
  - "Somewhat" is cut as a stacked second hedge, flagged below.
  - "In the existing literature" is cut because it restates what the keeper already says.
- **Terminology.** "The question of whether the identity of reviewers" became "whether reviewer identity": one term for one construct, and shorter.
- **Data sentence.** "In order to investigate this question, we leverage a novel dataset comprising of 1.2 million reviews" became "To investigate this question, we use a dataset of 1.2 million reviews".
  - "Leverage" becomes "use".
  - "Novel" is cut as a banned promotional adjective.
  - "Comprising of" is fixed.
  - The opening clause picks up "question" from the previous paragraph's last sentence, which keeps the given-new chain.
  - The number is unchanged.
- **Result sentence.** "The results of our analysis demonstrate that reviewer identity has a significant effect" became "Reviewer identity has a significant effect on this relationship." This removes the announcing frame and puts the finding in the subject slot. A short sentence after a longer one varies the rhythm. "On this relationship" is a reader-orientation phrase taken from the question the paragraph just posed, and it is flagged below.
- **Implication sentence.** "It should be noted that this finding has important implications for platform design: managers should consider highlighting reviewer credentials" became "Platform managers should therefore consider highlighting reviewer credentials."
  - This removes a banned hedging phrase and the announced "important implications".
  - The paragraph now ends on the consequence, with "therefore" naming the relation to the finding.
  - "For platform design" is folded into "Platform managers" and logged as a cut.
- **Sentence split.** Paragraph 1 sentence 2 is now split into two sentences, one topic each, with "studies" carrying the given-new chain.
- **Hedge census** over the high-cost zone:
  - No may, might, could, suggest, potentially, arguably, generally, typically or "in many cases".
  - Hits: "to the best of our knowledge" (epistemic keeper, kept), "somewhat" (filler, cut), "should consider" (recommendation hedge, kept as the claim's one hedge).

## 4. Author questions

- **Wording of the 5-9% claim.** "A one-star increase leads to a 5-9% increase in revenue" became "a one-star increase raises revenue by 5-9%". The numbers are unchanged, but please confirm the new wording keeps the quantity as you intended (percent change in revenue per one-star increase).
- **Source of the 5-9% range.** Do all three cited studies (Chevalier and Mayzlin 2006; Forman et al. 2008; Dellarocas 2003) report the 5-9% range, or is it a summary across them? The text attributes it to "these studies", and I cannot verify that.
- **Causal wording of the prior studies.** "Leads to" and "raises" are causal. Do those studies support a causal reading, or should this say "is associated with"?
- **"Somewhat".** Do you want it restored? It was a second hedge on the gap claim, and removing it slightly strengthens the claim. "To the best of our knowledge" still bounds it.
- **"On this relationship".** I added it to the result sentence. The original left the object of the effect open, and I took it from the question the paragraph posed. Is the effect on the strength of the review-sales relationship, on sales directly, or on something else?
- **Size and direction of the effect.** What are the direction and size of the identity effect? Without a magnitude beside the 5-9% baseline, the reader cannot judge what "significant effect" means. Is "significant" statistical, substantive, or both?
- **Results section.** Does it state the identity effect in full (estimate, units, uncertainty)? I could not verify that this early claim is borne out from the supplied text.
- **Identity versus credentials.** Are "reviewer identity" and "reviewer credentials" the same construct? If credentials are one component of identity, where is that defined for the reader?
- **"Novel" dataset.** What makes it new (source, platform, coverage, linkage to sales)? Naming that in a few words would restore the point without the adjective.
- **"Platform design".** The original said the finding "has important implications for platform design". Should the sentence name platform design explicitly, or is "Platform managers" enough?
- **Causal or correlational.** Is the claim that identity affects the relationship causal or correlational? "Has a significant effect" reads as causal. Does the design support that reading?
