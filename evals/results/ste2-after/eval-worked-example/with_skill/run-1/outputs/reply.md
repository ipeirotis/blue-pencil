## Revised text

```
The proliferation of online platforms has transformed how consumers make purchasing decisions. Several studies investigated the relationship between online reviews and sales (Chevalier and Mayzlin 2006; Forman et al. 2008; Dellarocas 2003). These studies found that reviews matter: a one-star increase leads to a 5-9% increase in revenue. However, to our knowledge, whether reviewer identity affects this relationship remains somewhat underexplored in the literature.

To investigate this question, we use a dataset of 1.2 million reviews. In our analysis, reviewer identity has a significant effect. This finding has implications for platform design: managers should consider highlighting reviewer credentials.
```

## Change rationale

The text goes from about 130 to about 90 words (-30%). The numbers (one-star, 5-9%, 1.2 million) and the three citations, in your original order, are unchanged.

- **Openers and transitions:** I removed "It is important to note," "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Crucially," and "It should be noted." I also dropped "fundamentally," "important," and "existing," and shortened "the way in which" to "how."
- **Verbs:** "An investigation ... was conducted by several researchers" became "Several studies investigated," which also matches the next sentence's "These studies." "Leverage" became "use."
- **Comma splice:** It is now a colon, which is a habit you already use elsewhere.
- **Gap sentence:** I moved "to our knowledge" to the front so the verb and its complement sit together. "Reviewer identity" now appears in one form. I kept both hedges.
- **Data sentence:** "In order to" became "To," and "comprising of" became "of." I cut "novel" because nothing in the text says what makes the data new.
- **Result sentence:** "The results of our analysis demonstrate that" became "In our analysis," and "significant" stays.
- **Not changed:** I left the paragraph order alone, and I did not merge sales/revenue or identity/credentials.
