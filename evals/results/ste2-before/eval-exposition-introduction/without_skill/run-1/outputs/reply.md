**Short answer:** it assumes a bit too much. A Management Science reader outside this subliterature will hit three gaps:

1. **The object of study is never named.** "The effect" appears without saying the effect of what on what. Your thesis says valence on sales, so I've stated that up front.
2. **The endogeneity problem is asserted, not explained.** One clause on why valence is correlated with the error term makes it legible to non-specialists.
3. **The logic from moderation to identification is compressed.** "Quasi-random assignment" is left hanging, so I've added a short clause saying what is being assigned.

I kept your claims, voice, and the 3.4 million figure. The text had no citations, so none changed.

```
The endogeneity of review valence with respect to unobserved product quality is a
well-known obstacle to identifying the causal effect of valence on sales in this
literature: the same unobserved quality that leads consumers to rate a product
highly also leads them to buy it. We exploit platform-level heterogeneity in
content moderation as a source of exogenous variation. Specifically, we leverage
the fact that platforms differentially apply moderation policies across review
cohorts, so that comparable reviews are moderated differently. This generates
quasi-random assignment, conditional on observed covariates, of the moderation
regime a review faces. Using a dataset of 3.4 million reviews, we estimate the
effect of review valence on sales and find it to be positive and significant. Our
identification strategy improves upon prior approaches that rely on instrumental
variables of questionable validity.
```

Two things I left alone but you may want to address in revision:

- **Tension between "exogenous" and "quasi-random conditional on covariates."** The first is stronger than the second. An economist reader may flag it, so consider using one framing consistently.
- **"Positive and significant" gives no magnitude.** Your numbers are off-limits to me, but an intro for this audience usually states the effect size in one phrase.
