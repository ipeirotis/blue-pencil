Mostly clear, but it assumes too much in three places:

- **"The effect" is never named.** A reader doesn't learn until later that it's the effect of review valence on sales.
- **The endogeneity problem is stated, not explained.** Economists will get it immediately, but IS readers may not, so I added one clause on why unobserved quality confounds valence and sales.
- **"Quasi-random assignment" doesn't say what is assigned.** I made it explicit that the assignment is to differing moderation exposure. Please check that this matches your design.

I changed no numbers or claims, and the draft has no citations.

```text
The endogeneity of review valence with respect to unobserved product quality is a
well-known obstacle to identification in this literature: higher-quality products
tend to receive more favorable reviews and also to sell more, so the observed
relationship between valence and sales cannot be read as causal. We exploit
platform-level heterogeneity in content moderation as a source of exogenous
variation. Specifically, we leverage the fact that platforms differentially apply
moderation policies across review cohorts, which generates quasi-random assignment
of cohorts to differing moderation exposure, conditional on observed covariates.
Using a dataset of 3.4 million reviews, we estimate the effect of review valence
on sales and find it to be positive and significant. Our identification strategy
improves upon prior approaches that rely on instrumental variables of
questionable validity.
```

Two things I left alone because fixing them would mean adding content:

- **Magnitude.** "Positive and significant" gives no effect size. Management Science readers will want one in the opening, if you can add it.
- **The final sentence.** It criticizes prior IV approaches without naming any. A citation or a brief example there would make the claim more convincing.
