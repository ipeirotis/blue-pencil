## Revised text

```
The proliferation of online platforms has transformed how consumers make purchasing decisions. Several researchers have investigated the relationship between online reviews and sales (Chevalier and Mayzlin 2006; Forman et al. 2008; Dellarocas 2003). These studies found that reviews matter: a one-star increase leads to a 5-9% increase in revenue. However, to our knowledge, whether reviewer identity affects this relationship remains underexplored in the literature.

To investigate this question, we use a dataset of 1.2 million reviews. In our analysis, reviewer identity has a significant effect on this relationship. This finding has implications for platform design: managers should consider highlighting reviewer credentials.
```

## Change rationale

The text goes from about 140 words to about 100 (-29%). I didn't change any number, citation, or citation order.

- **Opening sentence:** I cut "It is important to note that," "fundamentally," and "the way in which," so the claim opens the paragraph.
- **Citation sentence:** It is now active, with "Several researchers" as the subject. The citation order is unchanged.
- **Findings sentence:** A colon replaces the comma splice.
- **Gap sentence:** "Crucially" and the nominalised "the question of whether the identity of reviewers" are gone, and the terminology is now "reviewer identity." I cut "somewhat" and "existing." I compressed the hedge to "to our knowledge" and moved it to the front, so the verb and its complement sit together.
- **Data sentence:** "In order to investigate ... leverage a novel dataset comprising of" became "To investigate ... use a dataset of." I dropped "novel" as a promotional adjective.
- **Finding sentence:** "The results of our analysis demonstrate that" became "In our analysis." I added "on this relationship" to give "effect" an object.
- **Last sentence:** "It should be noted that" and "important" are gone, so the paragraph ends on the prescription.

## Author questions

- **Causal wording:** You write that a star increase "leads to" a 5-9% revenue increase and attribute that to all three studies. Does each study support causal language, and does the 5-9% range come from all three?
- **"Significant":** Does it mean statistically significant? If so, consider adding a direction and magnitude in the introduction to parallel the 5-9%.
- **"On this relationship":** I added it. Is the effect of identity on how much reviews move sales what you mean, or is it an effect on sales or ratings directly?
- **"Somewhat":** Cutting it makes the gap claim slightly stronger. Do you want the weaker wording back?
- **"Novel":** I removed it. If novelty matters, what is new about the data, so it can be stated as a fact?
- **Construct:** Do "reviewer identity" and "reviewer credentials" name the same thing? If not, what does identity cover in your data, and which parts should platforms highlight?
- **Named idea:** What phrase should readers carry away as the paper's core idea? The introduction currently offers only the topic.
- **Citation for the opening claim:** Does the claim that platforms transformed purchasing decisions need one? I added none.
