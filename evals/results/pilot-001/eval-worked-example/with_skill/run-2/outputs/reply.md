## Diagnosis

- **Voice tics:** You use "we" for your own work and impersonal phrasing for prior work. Citations sit in a parenthetical at the end of a sentence. The novelty claim is calibrated ("to the best of our knowledge"). A colon leads into the managerial implication, and the recommendation is plain ("managers should consider").
- **Reader map:** The reader starts out knowing that platforms matter and that reviews move sales. They need to learn that reviewer identity changes how much a review moves sales. They should leave thinking platforms should surface reviewer credentials.
- **Jargon:** "Reviewer identity" appears four times and is never defined or illustrated. "Reviewer credentials" shows up at the end as if it meant the same thing.
- **Buried lede:** The finding appears only as "a significant effect", with no object, direction or size.
- **Concrete anchor:** The only concrete detail is the one-star, 5-9% revenue figure. Nothing shows what "reviewer identity" looks like, and I can't add an example without inventing one.

1. **Opening sentence:** It is a generic claim, and the puzzle (does identity matter?) doesn't arrive until sentence 4. The spine holds: platforms matter, reviews move sales, nobody has asked about identity, so we test it. I kept the order and compressed the opener.
2. **Connectives:** "Furthermore", "Moreover" and "Crucially" were carrying the flow in place of the content. I rebuilt the chain as cited studies, then their finding, then "however".
3. **Comma splice:** In "reviews matter, a one-star increase leads to…", the second clause explains the first, so a colon fits. The causal "leads to" is your claim, so I flagged it.
4. **Gap sentence:** The hedge interrupted the verb and its complement, and "in the existing literature" repeated what "underexplored" already says.
5. **Referents:** "This relationship" crossed a paragraph break, and "a significant effect" had no object. I supplied the object from the preceding paragraph: the relationship between reviews and sales.
6. **Sentence-level problems:** "In order to", "leverage", "comprising of", "novel", "The results of our analysis demonstrate that", "It should be noted that" and "important".
7. **No takeaway phrase:** The opening gives a topic and a finding but nothing to carry away. Your thesis (identity, not just the rating, shapes how much a review moves sales) is sharper than "significant effect". I routed this to the author questions.

## Revised text

```
The proliferation of online platforms has transformed how consumers make purchasing decisions. Several researchers have studied how online reviews relate to sales (Chevalier and Mayzlin 2006; Forman et al. 2008; Dellarocas 2003). These studies found that reviews matter: a one-star increase leads to a 5-9% increase in revenue. To the best of our knowledge, however, whether reviewer identity affects this relationship remains somewhat underexplored.

To investigate this question, we use a dataset of 1.2 million reviews. In our data, reviewer identity has a significant effect on the relationship between reviews and sales. This finding has implications for platform design: managers should consider highlighting reviewer credentials.
```
