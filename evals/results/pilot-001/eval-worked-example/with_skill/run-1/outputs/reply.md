## Revised text

```
The proliferation of online platforms has transformed how consumers make purchasing decisions. Several studies have investigated the relationship between online reviews and sales (Chevalier and Mayzlin 2006; Forman et al. 2008; Dellarocas 2003). These studies found that reviews matter: a one-star increase leads to a 5-9% increase in revenue. However, to the best of our knowledge, whether reviewer identity affects this relationship remains underexplored.

To investigate this question, we use a dataset of 1.2 million reviews. Our analysis finds that reviewer identity has a significant effect on this relationship. This finding has implications for platform design: managers should consider highlighting reviewer credentials.
```

The text drops from about 135 to about 100 words. The numbers (5-9%, 1.2 million), the citations and their order are unchanged.

## Change rationale

- **Cut:** the opening filler and "fundamentally," "important," "novel" and "Crucially." "Somewhat" and "in the existing literature" are also cut, which I flag below.
- **Rewrote:** "an investigation … was conducted by several researchers" became "Several studies have investigated…", so "These studies" now has an antecedent. "Leverage" became "use." "The results of our analysis demonstrate" became "Our analysis finds."
- **Fixed:** the comma splice, now a colon, and "comprising of."
- **Unified:** "reviewer identity" is now the single term.
- **Kept:** "to the best of our knowledge," as the one hedge on the gap claim, and "managers should consider."
- **Declined:** reordering to open on the puzzle, and merging sentences 2 and 3, since either would change your framing or move the citations.

## Author questions

1. Do all three cited studies report the 5-9% figure, or is it your synthesis? Is "leads to" their causal wording, and is "revenue" the same construct as "sales" in the sentence before?
2. Does "significant" mean statistically significant? What are the direction and magnitude, ideally set against the 5-9% baseline? Is the design strong enough for "effect" to read causally?
3. I added "on this relationship" to the finding sentence. Is that what you find: an effect on how much a review moves sales?
4. Is "reviewer identity" the same construct as "reviewer credentials"? The passage never defines it, and it is your central term.
5. I cut "novel." What makes the 1.2 million reviews distinctive (platform, period, categories)? That could be a sentence of its own.
6. I cut "somewhat" and "in the existing literature." Does the gap claim still say what you intend?
7. Does the body support the managerial implication and state the full conditions of the headline finding?
8. Is there one phrase you want readers to carry away, such as "identity, not just the rating"? The opening has a topic and a result but no handle, and I didn't invent one.
