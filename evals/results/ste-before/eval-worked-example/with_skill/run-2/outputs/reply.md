## Diagnosis

The draft reads as a flat list joined by "Furthermore" and "Moreover". The tension only shows at "however", and the gap that motivates your paper sits at the end of a hedged 28-word sentence. The finding that identity matters arrives late, behind throat-clearing. At the first-draft stage, structural moves are allowed. Your numbers, citations and citation order are untouched.

Sentence-level problems found:
- **Throat-clearing:** "It is important to note that" and "It should be noted that".
- **Transitions:** "Furthermore", "Moreover" and "Crucially".
- **Passive nominalization:** "an investigation … was conducted by".
- **Wordiness:** "the way in which" and "In order to".
- **Tell words:** "leverage", "novel", "demonstrate" and "fundamentally".
- **Grammar:** "comprising of".
- **Comma splice:** the one before "a one-star increase".
- **Term drift:** "identity of reviewers" and "reviewer identity".
- **Hedging:** "somewhat", "to the best of our knowledge" and "in the existing literature" stack on one claim.

## Revised text

```
The proliferation of online platforms has transformed how consumers make
purchasing decisions. Several studies have examined how online reviews relate
to sales (Chevalier and Mayzlin 2006; Forman et al. 2008; Dellarocas 2003).
These studies found that reviews matter: a one-star increase leads to a 5-9%
increase in revenue. Yet, to the best of our knowledge, whether reviewer
identity affects this relationship remains underexplored.

To investigate this question, we use a dataset of 1.2 million reviews. We find
that reviewer identity has a significant effect on this relationship. Platform
managers should therefore consider highlighting reviewer credentials.
```

## Change rationale

The text goes from about 140 to about 90 words (-36%). The reviser loaded the full set of passes for an introduction at first draft and skipped none. The whole-paper passes (cold-read, consistency-checks) don't apply to a single section.

- **Opener:** I cut "It is important to note that", "fundamentally" and "the way in which". The topic is now the subject of the sentence.
- **Studies sentence:** The passive "an investigation … was conducted by several researchers" is now "Several studies have examined…". The next sentence's "These studies" picks it up, which carries the flow.
- **Findings sentence:** I dropped "Moreover" and replaced the comma splice with a colon.
- **Gap sentence:**
  - "Crucially, however" became "Yet".
  - I moved the hedge to the front so subject and verb sit together.
  - I cut "somewhat" and "in the existing literature". "To the best of our knowledge" stays as the one hedge.
- **Dataset sentence:** "In order to … leverage a novel dataset comprising of" became "To … use a dataset of". I removed "novel" because it was promotional.
- **Result sentence:** "The results of our analysis demonstrate" became "We find", and I added "on this relationship".
- **Implication sentence:** I deleted "It should be noted that" and "important implications for platform design", since your recommendation already shows the consequence. I added "therefore" to link the finding to the recommendation.

Three edits go slightly beyond tightening and are worth checking: "on this relationship", "therefore", and removing "novel". Each is also raised below.

## Author questions

1. Do all three cited studies report the one-star, 5-9% revenue result, or does it come from one study or a synthesis? If it's one study, should its citation move to that sentence?
2. Do those studies support the causal "leads to", or should it read as an association?
3. Does "significant" mean statistically significant? Can you add the direction and magnitude, since the introduction currently states neither?
4. Is the effect on how much a review moves sales (your core thesis) or a direct effect on sales? I added "on this relationship" on the first reading.
5. What counts as "reviewer identity" in your data, and is "reviewer credentials" the same construct or a subset?
6. Does your evidence support recommending that platforms highlight credentials? "Therefore" asserts that link, where your original left it to a colon.
7. What made the dataset "novel" (platform, identity information, sales measure)? Which platform and sales measure should readers see here?
8. Do any prior studies partly address reviewer identity? Since I cut "somewhat", should the sentence name the closest ones and state the contrast?
9. Why would identity matter (credibility, similarity, something else)? The opening gives no mechanism, and your thesis ("not just the rating") doesn't appear in this excerpt.
10. Does the body state the identity effect with its scope and conditions, so the unconditional claim here holds up? I couldn't check that from the excerpt.
